01 — Approach

How I think about quality.

Finding bugs is the easy part. The real work is knowing which risks matter, proving the product does what was promised, and deciding when it's ready.

01

Test by risk, not by habit.

Not every feature carries the same weight. I put testing effort where a failure would hurt the business most.

In practiceRisk management and impact-based prioritisation on every CR.

02

Requirements are the contract.

If a requirement isn't traced to a test, nobody has actually checked it. I keep that link visible from start to finish.

In practiceA Requirement Traceability Matrix at 95–100% coverage.

03

A release is a decision, not a date.

Shipping on schedule only matters if what ships works. I make the call on evidence and stand behind it.

In practiceI own go/no-go decisions and post-release checks.

04

Good QA makes the team faster.

Done well, QA speeds the team up. Repeatable environments and a well-mentored team mean shorter cycles and fewer surprises.

In practiceQA environments on Azure DevOps CI/CD, and a team of 3 I mentor.

03 — Process

How a release earns its “go”.

Six stages I run on every project, from the first change request to the checks after it's live.

  1. 01✓

    Plan

    Estimate the change, map the risks, and decide what deserves the most attention.

  2. 02✓

    Design

    Test plans, scenarios and cases, each traced back to a requirement.

  3. 03✓

    Execute

    Functional, regression, integration, API, database and performance testing on web, desktop and mobile.

  4. 04✓

    Track

    Every defect logged, prioritised and followed through to a fix in Jira.

  5. 05✓

    Accept

    UAT with the client, taken through to formal sign-off.

  6. 06✓

    Release

    The go/no-go call, deployment through Azure DevOps, then verification in production.
